# Writing style

## Commits

Subject: `type: summary`. Imperative mood, 60 characters or fewer, no period.

Types: `feat`, `fix`, `refactor`, `test`, `docs`, `chore`, `deps`.

Body: what the change does. Present tense. Wrap at 80 characters.

Leave out:

- Trailers such as `Co-Authored-By` or `Signed-off-by`.
- Attribution to a person or a tool.
- Commentary and notes to the reader.
- History. Do not say what the code did before.
- Selling words such as "comprehensive" or "robust".

## Pull requests

Title: same rules as a commit subject.

Description: what the change does and why. Under 15 lines. Use a list for
several parts.

Leave out test plans, checklists, and diff summaries.

## Prose

Applies to READMEs, docs, code comments, PR bodies, and chat replies.

- One idea per sentence. 20 words for an instruction, 25 for a description.
- Active voice. Present tense.
- One meaning per word. No synonym for a term you already used.
- No metaphor, idiom, or slang.
- Finite verbs, not gerunds or participle phrases.
- Keep "the" and "a". Noun clusters of 3 words or fewer.
- Positive statements. Instructions as commands.
- Paragraphs of 6 sentences or fewer. Lists for sequences and sets.
- Keep technical names and verbs as they are.
- US spelling.

## Code comments

Match the comment density of the file. Say why, not what.
